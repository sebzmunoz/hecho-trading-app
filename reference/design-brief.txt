Hecho Trading Co. · Mobile App · Design Brief · v1.1
The brief I'll use to design every screen of the functional prototype .
A phone-first wholesale companion to Hecho's web platform, built around the physical showroom. This document fixes scope, decisions, architecture, screens, flows, and states, so screen design begins the moment it is approved.
Persona focus
4
Buyer, assistant, rep, brand owner.
Screens to design
91
Five tabs, plus onboarding, rep mode, system, edge states.
Flows clickable
14
Every primary motion, end to end, with branches.
Success criterion
1
A buyer scans a real showroom shelf and submits a reorder without leaving the app.
01 · Audience & scope
Four people. One mobile app. One scope.
The brief targets a single app that serves three of the four personas (P1, P2, P3). The brand-side experience (P4) is its own product and is out of scope for this round. Each persona below is self-contained: role, goals, pains, usage rhythm, and the basis I am working from.
P1 · Primary
The Boutique Buyer
Owner-operator of 1 to 10 gift, home, and lifestyle stores. Walks the showroom on visit days and approves every final order personally. Holds the budget and the brand relationships.
Goals
Never run out of bestsellers; never sit on dead stock; move from a real shelf to a draft cart in seconds; keep final sign-off in my own hands.
Pains
Reordering means cross-checking shelves against spreadsheets; live stock is invisible on the floor; neighbors can read my numbers over my shoulder; approving a manager's cart still needs a phone call.
Frequency
A few buying trips per season, plus reorders between trips; opens the app weekly to check orders and approvals.
Basis Assumption from v0.7 product framing, not yet validated by direct buyer research.
P2 · Secondary
The Buying Assistant
Store manager employed by P1. Walks the showroom on the owner's behalf and builds draft carts, but cannot submit a final order alone. Maps to the Manager company role.
Goals
Build the draft cart on the floor; send it up for approval without a phone call; get the green light fast; cover more of the showroom than the owner has time for.
Pains
No authority to commit spend, so every cart stalls waiting on the owner; cannot self-approve; loses context when handing a cart over; unclear which lines the owner will cut.
Frequency
Most active during the owner's buying trips and between-trip reorders; in the app several days a week when a season is running.
Basis Assumption from v0.7 product framing, not yet validated by direct buyer research.
P3 · Internal
The Hecho Rep
Hecho's own field rep. Walks the showroom alongside a buyer, co-shops draft carts live, and switches between assigned retailer accounts. Quotes credit, shipping, and MOQ on the spot. Maps to the Rep role.
Goals
Co-shop a draft cart with my buyer and have it ready before the next appointment; keep every assigned retailer moving; add the right line the moment the buyer points at a shelf.
Pains
Cannot submit on a retailer's behalf without an explicit grant; juggles many accounts in one day; must respect each retailer's privacy setting; appointments overlap and carts go cold between visits.
Frequency
Daily during showroom season; multiple retailer accounts touched per visit day.
Basis Assumption from v0.7 product framing, not yet validated by direct buyer research.
P4 · Out of scope
The Brand Owner
Maker or founder of a brand Hecho represents. Curates products, watches performance, and reads demand signals. Lives in a parallel brand-side app, not this one.
Goals
Curate the assortment Hecho shows; understand sell-through across retailers; time brand launches; surface the products worth pushing.
Pains
No direct line to retailer demand; launch timing is guesswork; authored style guides need approval before they ship. (All addressed in the brand-side product, not here.)
Frequency
Not applicable to this scope; the brand-side app is designed in a later round.
Basis Assumption from v0.7 product framing, not yet validated. Out of scope for v1.1.
Platform
Native iOS and Android, mobile-first. Tablet support is graceful (the same layout scales up; nothing tablet-exclusive). Web has its own product already in use; this app reaches parity with the web's retailer-facing features (cart, drafts, order history, address book, company users, AI upload-a-file, AI forward-an-email, scan-a-barcode, repeat-order) and adds the showroom-native layer (live shelf scan, live POS stock, smart reorder, privacy on the floor, rep co-shop, wayfinding). No public landing site or marketing surface is part of this scope.
Out of scope
The brand-side app (P4 surfaces), the admin desktop, the marketing site, AR shelf overlays, full localization, in-showroom tap-to-pay, and any payment processor beyond ACH and saved card. Itemized in §09 .

MC · Market context
The category is crowded. The floor is empty.
Wholesale ordering is a solved-looking category: Faire, NuOrder, Joor, and Brandboom all move line sheets and Net terms at scale. None of them was built around a buyer standing in a physical showroom with a phone. That gap is the one thing I own, and it is the reason this app is mobile-first, not a port.
The field
Who I am measured against
Faire. Marketplace discovery and Net terms for small retail. Web and app, no showroom layer.
NuOrder. Brand-led digital line sheets and B2B order writing. Sales-rep tooling, desktop-anchored.
Joor. Enterprise wholesale and virtual showrooms. Virtual, not the physical floor.
Brandboom. Line sheets, order capture, a lightweight catalog. No live POS, no on-floor privacy.
The one differentiator
What none of them can do
I am showroom-native: a live shelf scan that resolves to that retailer's own live POS stock, on the floor. Two things ride only on that floor: Privacy on the floor (sensitive price and stock masked by default, revealed only while the buyer holds the chip, per F12) and rep co-shop (a Hecho rep adding to the buyer's draft live, per F11).
That triple, live-POS scan plus privacy plus co-shop, is the position I defend. A desktop-born competitor cannot retrofit it.
Table stakes
Parity I must reach to be taken seriously
Line sheets. A curated, browsable surface. I answer this with the style-guide surface (S001, S002), a spread not a spreadsheet.
Net terms. Net-30 ordering with a credit relationship. Covered by the wallet and the Net-terms model in §02 Q3.
Reorder. Repeat-order off past purchases as a first-class motion. Covered by Smart reorder (F6).
Read
Where the line sits
Parity is the cost of entry, not the pitch. I match line sheets, Net terms, and reorder so a buyer never feels they traded down to come to the floor. I win on the floor itself. If the showroom layer is not unmistakably better than typing into Faire back at the store, I have built a worse Faire. The whole brief leans the other way.

Q1
POS integrations, top three Inferred · OI-11
Shopify, Lightspeed, Square at launch. Open API for everything else. Retailers without a connected POS still use every feature; live stock fields degrade to "unknown, last counted" with a manual count entry.
Design implication The S413 Connected POS screen ships with three vendor cards plus an "Other (open API)" entry. Every .scan-result , product detail, and reorder card has a degraded state for "POS not connected" that swaps the stock number for a manual-count field. POS-disconnected retailers reach every other feature without friction.
Q2
One app or two Confirmed
One app, role-switching . P1, P2, P3 share a single binary with role pickers on launch and a quick switch in the You tab. P4 (brand-side) gets its own app in a later round.
Design implication An account picker ( S506 ) appears at first launch when the account holds more than one role. Role switching is reachable from S401 You as a one-tap row. The bottom tab bar adapts: P3 (rep) gets a Retailers slot in place of You ; navigation labels and content lists are role-aware.
Q3
In-app payment scope Confirmed
Net-30 ACH and saved-card auto-pay at launch. Tap-to-pay is deferred. Every order opens as Net-30 by default; the buyer can override to "pay now" with a saved instrument.
Design implication The Wallet sits inside the Orders tab ( S304 Payment sheet ) and surfaces from order detail and the invoice viewer. ACH onboarding is a one-time flow ( S304a ) gated to the first ACH payment, not at signup. No in-showroom checkout component is designed in this round.
Q3+
Net-terms, completed: source, limit, over-limit, past-due Inferred defaults
Q3 locked Net-30 ACH and saved card , with partial payment not supported in v1.0 (the amount is fixed). What follows fills the four gaps the wallet needs to be buildable.
Terms source An account carries a default term, Net-30 , settable per account by Hecho ops . The buyer never edits it. It drives the due window on every invoice and the wallet on S301 .
Credit limit Each account has a credit limit, set by Hecho ops, shown in the wallet as a balance-against-limit row (outstanding, limit, headroom). It is a Privacy-on-the-floor sensitive field: masked by default, revealed only on hold.
Over-limit If a submit would push outstanding past the limit, S204 shows an over-limit warning row above Submit. It does not hard-block the submit in v1.0. Inferred default, pending confirmation.
Past-due An account with any invoice past its due window surfaces a wallet chip on S301 and a coral "Past due" tag (icon plus label plus color, never color alone) on the affected order rows. Past-due does not hard-block new submits in v1.0. Inferred default, flag for confirmation.
Q4
Pricing visibility rules Inferred · OI-12
Negotiated wholesale + MAP / MSRP visible to every authenticated retailer . Volume-tier prices appear only on lines where the buyer qualifies. Tier-locked prices show a generic "Higher tier" hint, never the dollar number.
Design implication The .price component renders three rows where relevant: Your price (always), MSRP as a strike-through ref, Volume tier visible only to qualifiers, masked otherwise. Tier-gated brands hide their pricing entirely behind S804 Tier-locked . The privacy mask ( F12 ) hides Your price first.
Q5
Style-guide authorship Confirmed
Hecho and brand owners both author guides . A brand-owner guide that contains another brand's product enters an approval queue and only ships once Hecho approves. The retailer always sees a single curated surface and never knows who authored a guide.
Design implication The retailer-facing surface ( S001 Shop home , S002 Style guide detail , S705 Gallery ) is identical regardless of author. The brand-owner authoring + approval UI is a P4 surface and lives outside this scope. The brief shows the consuming side only and does not expose author metadata on the retailer screens.
Q6
Brand exclusivity tiers Inferred · OI-13
Yes, tiers exist . Some brands are tier-gated, hidden from non-qualifying retailers. New brand launches give a top-tier first-look window; mid-tier and standard retailers see the brand only after the window closes.
Design implication Discovery surfaces filter by the buyer's tier silently. Direct deep links to a tier-locked brand land on S804 Tier-locked . Push delivery for brand launches ( F14 ) is segmented by tier upstream; the design assumes the notification reaches only eligible retailers.

TM · Tier model
Two axes. One buildable rule per axis.
Tiers are two different things wearing one word. The first is a volume tier : a price break the buyer earns per line. The second is an exclusivity tier : which brands a retailer is allowed to see at all. They never share a predicate, so I define each separately and render each differently. Every predicate below is an Inferred default, pending confirmation .
A
Volume tier: a per-line quantity break
A buyer qualifies for a volume tier on a single line when that line's quantity meets or exceeds the brand's published break for the tier. The predicate is local to the line, not the cart, not the account: line qty &ge; brand break(tier) . Cross-line and cross-brand bundling is out of scope in v1.0.
Render rule When a higher break is in reach, the masked volume price shows the unlock hint "Save at {n} units" , where {n} is the brand's break for the next tier. I never print the dollar figure of a price the buyer has not unlocked. The moment the line crosses {n} , the unlocked price replaces the hint inline on S004 and S102 . When the buyer does not qualify and no break is in reach, the row falls back to the generic "Higher tier" hint from Q4, never the dollar number.
B
Exclusivity tier: assigned, not computed
Exclusivity has three named levels: Standard , Mid , Top . A retailer's level is assigned by Hecho ops , not derived from spend or volume. The app reads the level as account state. It governs which brands are visible, which are first-look eligible, and which are locked.
Render rule Discovery surfaces filter silently by the account's level. A brand the level cannot see is never teased on an index. A direct deep link to a brand above the level lands on S804 Tier-locked, the same lock F14's below-tier branch routes to on Brand index and Brand page, with the CTA Request access.

S804
OI-06 resolved · upgrade path
From Request access to a brand that just appears
The path OI-06 left open, made concrete and reversible at each step:
1. Request access. On S804 the buyer taps Request access. The button emits brand.requested_access and the screen moves to a Request sent state with the brand held.
2. Rep follow-up. The request surfaces to the assigned P3 rep, who confirms fit with the buyer over rep chat.
3. Ops grant. Hecho ops raises the account's exclusivity level, or grants the single brand. Same assignment lever as axis B: a human action, not a computed unlock.
4. Resolves on next load. On the next load the brand resolves to its normal state. S804 is replaced by the live Brand page. No celebratory interstitial: the lock simply is not there anymore. Owner: Hecho ops .

05 · User flows
Fourteen flows. Every screen plays a part.
Each flow is the end-to-end path a persona takes from intent to outcome, with screen IDs in brackets at every step. A single screen may appear in many flows. Branching paths are called out inline.
F1
First-time setup and onboarding
All personas
From cold install to a usable home screen, with optional POS connect and compliance staging.
Open the app for the first time. S501 Splash holds the brand mark during cold boot.
S502 Welcome. Three swipeable cards (Scan, Draft, Reorder). Tap Sign in, or Skip to sign in.
S503 Sign in. Enter email for a magic link, or pick SSO (Apple, Google, Hecho).
S504 Magic link sent. Open the link on this device, or resend.
The link returns to the app. S505 Email verification signs the buyer in.
If the account holds more than one retailer account or a rep role, S506 Account picker appears. Single-role accounts skip it.
S507 Camera permission prompt, with rationale before the OS prompt. Allow or Skip.
S508 Notification permission prompt, with a category preview. Allow or Skip.
S509 POS connect invite (skippable). Pick Shopify, Lightspeed, or Square to jump to S414 POS OAuth flow, then back; or skip and return later from the You tab.
Land on S001 Shop home. The first Scan tap shows the S101 first-run Coachmark. On arrival at a showroom, S510 First showroom-visit cue offers wayfinding.
If Tax-ID has an open action, a soft chip on S401 You points to S409 . Not blocking until Submit.
Branch · invite path If the buyer came from an invite link, S503 skips email entry and the token carries through S505 to S506 Account picker, pre-attached to the inviting retailer.
F2
Walking the showroom and scanning a real shelf
P1, P2
The signature in-person motion. Camera on, real product, live stock, instant add. A rep co-shopping (F11) reuses this same scan motion in the active retailer's context.
Tap Scan in the tab bar. S101 Scanner viewfinder opens in barcode mode by default.
Point at a barcode on a real shelf. The viewfinder locks.
S102 Scan result appears as a half-sheet over the camera: product, brand, wholesale, MSRP, MAP chip, stock at the buyer store (live POS or last cached), last order date and qty, recommended reorder quantity.
Tap Add to cart. S211 Add to cart sheet opens. Pick a draft (or new draft), set quantity, confirm.
Sheet collapses; viewfinder live again with a confirmation chip ("Added to Back wall refresh").
Optionally open the floor map from the Scanner header.
Branch · camera denied If permission is denied, S105 appears with a Manual SKU entry shortcut to S106 .
F3
Photo-identifying a product from a competitor or magazine
P1, P2
Same Scanner, photo mode. Multi-match disambiguation, soft fallback to AI ingest.
Open S101 Scanner viewfinder, switch to photo mode. S103 Photo recognition opens (capture, or pick from library).
Take a photo of the product on a competitor shelf or magazine page.
S104 Photo result presents up to five candidates ranked by confidence with brand chips and price.
Tap a candidate. Lands on S004 Product detail.
Add to cart via S211 Add to cart sheet; return to scanner or jump to the draft via cart toast.
Branch · no match If no good match, the screen offers Forward an email and Upload a file shortcuts (existing web AI ingest).
F4
Building a named draft cart from scratch
P1, P2
From empty to ready, with multiple sources combining into one named draft. Drafts never expire.
Open Carts. S201 Carts index.
Tap New cart. S203 New cart sheet. Name it ("Back wall refresh"). Pick template (blank, repeat last, smart reorder).
Land on S202 Cart detail (empty).
Add lines from any source: S102 Scan result, S004 Product detail, S002 Style guide detail (via Shop the look), S006 Search results.
Each add goes through S211 Add to cart sheet (qty, destination). The destination defaults to the current cart.
Edit lines on S202 (swap variant, change qty, remove). Move lines between drafts via long-press.
Save and continue later from any screen.
Branch · MOQ not met If a brand minimum is not met, S207 MOQ-not-met state offers proposed additions from items the buyer has ordered before.
F5
Sharing a draft with a co-owner for approval
P1, P2
P2 builds, P1 approves, built on the existing Company Users sharing model.
P2 finishes a draft in S202 Cart detail. Tap Share.
S205 Cart share sheet opens. Pick a co-owner from S405 Company Users. Permission level: Can edit, or Approve to submit. Send.
S206 Share confirmation toast. P1 receives a push (category: Approval requests).

P1 approves to submit (jumps to S204 Cart submit) or sends back with notes (P2 receives a push; the draft returns to S202 with comments).

F6
Smart reorder from past orders
P1, P2
Past orders become starting points, not history. The behavior model proposes a draft cart; the buyer accepts piece by piece.
From S201 Carts index or S301 Orders index, tap Smart reorder. S203 opens with the Smart reorder template selected.
The Builder lists proposed lines, each with the why ("Sold X in 30 days at your store, last bought N months ago, season match").
Accept all, accept some, edit quantities inline. Each accepted line is added to a new draft.
Dead lines (no recent sell-through) are de-emphasized with a Remove suggestion.
The proposed draft cart totals and any MOQ flags surface at the bottom.
Tap Continue. Lands on the new draft in S202 , ready to share or submit.
Branch · no POS connected Suggestions are weighted by order history alone; a soft pitch invites the buyer to connect a POS for sharper recommendations.

06 · Per-screen specs
One card per screen. Every state surfaced.
Each card carries an ID anchor for deep-linking. Every component named on a card has one canonical entry: either in the Design System v1.0 (Foundations §01 and the Components roster §02), or in the Component Manifest below. The manifest is the spec for the additions Design System v1.1 must absorb before the screens that use them are built. Nothing load-bearing resolves by guesswork: every net-new composite has a manifest entry below, and every remaining name on a card is a configured instance of a documented Design System primitive (a picker, chip, row, button, illustration, or sheet), never a new undocumented component. Where a name here differs from the system, I reconcile it: the brief's Masked Field is the Design System Privacy mask ( .privacy-row ), revealed through its is-holding state, so I drop the invented "Reveal Layer" and render hold-to-preview as the Privacy mask holding, not a separate component.

A · Offline & sync
What stays usable. What can wait.
Local-first for browse, drafts, and notes. Writes queue and replay on reconnect. Submit, payment, RMA submit, and POS sync require network.
Persistent banner at the top of every screen when offline.
Sync-pending tag on each local draft, memo, message until acknowledged by the server.
Conflict resolution uses last-write-wins for non-cart fields. Cart-line conflicts route to S807 Sync conflict resolver.
Photo uploads compress on capture and resume on reconnect.

D · Privacy on the floor
What stays masked. How to reveal.
On by default after onboarding. Off-floor buyers can change in S412 .
Masked by default : negotiated wholesale, on-hand stock, past spend, credit balance, recommended reorder qty.
Reveal gesture : hold-to-preview (default). Double-tap is the accessibility fallback (auto-mask after five seconds). Off disables masking entirely.
Screenshot watermark overlays "for buyer eyes" when supported by the OS interceptor.
Quick toggle via header icon → S210 sheet.
E · Accessibility
Floors I hold on every screen.
I meet the floors documented in Design System v1.0 and add the screen-specific commitments below. Where the system and this brief once disagreed, this card is the source of truth.
Coral is decoration, never the only signal : I never set coral text below 18 px bold or 24 px regular, and never use coral as the sole focus indicator. Coral on Antique White is 4.12:1, so it clears large and icon use only.
Focus ring : Brand Gray by default, or a 3 px coral ring on a 1 px Brand-Gray inner halo so focus survives on any ground. I never strip :focus-visible .
Error text : Brand Gray on Surface paired with a coral alert icon. The icon carries color; the word carries meaning. Coral never sets the error sentence itself.
Contrast floors : 4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for large text and icon affordances. Brand Gray on Antique White (8.3:1) is my body default.
Status is icon plus label plus color, never color alone : check + "Current"; clock + "Renews soon"; alert-triangle + "Expired"; coral alert icon + "Past due". The triad replaces the old "(green/yellow/red)" shorthand everywhere.
Hit targets minimum 44 by 44 px; primary actions at 48. Reveal and icon-only controls extend the tappable area to 44 with transparent padding even when the visible glyph is 32.
Reveal mode is its own setting : Hold, Tap-to-toggle, Double-tap (5 s), or Always-off-floor. Default is Hold, and I auto-switch to Tap-to-toggle the moment VoiceOver, TalkBack, or Switch Control is active. Tap-to-toggle reveals on one activation and re-masks on the next, with no sustained pressure and no timing.
Reduced motion is decoupled from reveal : it removes animation only; it never changes the gesture choice.
Dynamic Type : I honor OS text scaling through iOS AX3 and Android 200%. No fixed-height text containers; the price pair, line row, and status row reflow from columns to stacked; the S416 table switches to stacked cards above the scale threshold.
Focus order follows visual order; modals trap focus. The S102 half-sheet over the camera traps focus and restores to the viewfinder on dismiss; the S807 diff announces "row N, Mine vs Theirs".
VoiceOver / TalkBack labels sit on every interactive element. The masked chip announces one canonical string everywhere: "Your price, hidden. Double-tap to reveal." See the screen-reader copy deck (§SR).
E&prime; · Inclusive showroom
Reading prices in a real booth, by any buyer.
The showroom is loud, bright, and shoulder-to-shoulder. These commitments keep a masked price legible and private for a buyer who scales text, mirrors layout, or works by voice and ear.
Reflow over truncation : when text scales up, every multi-column showroom row stacks rather than clips. Nothing hides behind a fixed height.
RTL with logical insets : I use logical start and end insets and mirror directional icons (chevron, back, stepper progression, breadcrumb). I never mirror the viewfinder, media, numerals, SKUs, or the HECHO mark.
Tap-to-toggle under assistive tech : with VoiceOver, TalkBack, or Switch Control on, reveal is a single rotor action ("Reveal value" / "Mask value") with no hold and no countdown.
Voice input respects the mask : when Privacy on the floor is ON, I suppress voice input for sensitive fields; in noise I fall back to keyboard plus barcode with a one-line hint.
Status that survives color blindness and glare : every status carries an icon and a word, so a buyer reads "Past due" and the alert glyph together, never the hue alone.
F · Localization
English-only in v1.0. Designed to translate.
Full localization is out of scope for this round. The design accepts variable string length and respects RTL-safe layouts.
String length sized for 1.4 times longest English expansion.
Numbers and currencies use the buyer account locale today; explicit i18n in a later version.
Date phrases are relative ("two days ago"), never specific.

07b · App visual direction
Four things the system does not yet rule on. Ruled on here.
Design System v1.0 settles color, type, spacing, and the documented components. It does not settle where real photography lives, whether the app goes dark, how the showroom map looks, or which motion patterns the screens lean on. I resolve all four here so the screen work has no open visual questions. Each rule that introduces a component points back to the Component Manifest .
A · Photography vs line-art
Editorial stays drawn. Product, capture, and proof use real photos.
The discovery system is line-art: flat-lay line drawings in one 1.5 px stroke, Terracotta on a paper ground, no fills or shading. That holds for every editorial surface. Real photography enters only where the buyer needs to see the actual thing, through a single Media component (manifest F1).
Line-art stays on Style guide detail, brand heroes, empty states, and section breaks.
Photography is required at product hero ( S004 ), photo-recognition capture ( S103 ), RMA damage photo ( S307 ), and PDF invoice preview ( S303 ).
Media component fixes the rules: aspect ratios (1:1 product, 4:5 capture, page ratio for invoice), corner radius from the DS radius scale, a loading placeholder, and an error placeholder.
RMA capture frame is a dedicated Media state: a framed live preview with a capture control, photos compressed on capture.
B · Dark mode
Light-only, except the camera. Full dark deferred.
The v1.0 position is explicit: the app is light-only. The one exception is the camera surfaces, which already render their controls over a dark camera feed using the Design System dark surfaces.
Light everywhere by default; the Antique White canvas is the ground for every non-camera screen.
Camera surfaces render dark : the viewfinder and its overlays at S101 , S102 , S103 draw controls over the live feed on the DS dark surfaces, so affordances stay legible on any shelf color.
Full dark mode is deferred to a Design System v1.1 item; I do not theme the rest of the app dark in this round.
C · Map & wayfinding
The floor map is net-new, with a no-location fallback.
Nothing in Design System v1.0 covers a map. The Floor Plan , Map Pin , and Breadcrumb (manifest G2 to G4) are net-new and ship into Design System v1.1 before S708 is built. The floor plan is drawn line-art, consistent with the illustration rules.
Floor Plan renders the showroom as a line illustration with the current booth highlighted.
No-location fallback : when location is unavailable, the surface degrades to a manual booth picker; an unknown showroom shows a generic booth grid.
Open dependency : the booth-coordinate source is unresolved. I cite OI-01 and will not finalize the Floor Plan geometry until it lands.
D · Motion
Three patterns, each mapped to a system token.
The brief leans on three motion patterns, and each maps to a Design System duration and ease rather than an improvised timing. Reduced motion disables them; fades remain.
Hero arrival : editorial and product heroes settle on --d-base (300 ms) with --e-out-expo .
Sheet slide : bottom sheets, payment, review surfaces rise on --d-slow (500 ms) with --e-out-expo .
Skeleton shimmer : loading skeletons ( S808 ) sweep continuously on --e-in-out .
Reduced motion disables hero arrival, sheet slide, and shimmer (the scan line goes static); fades remain so nothing depends on motion alone.
07-H · Data & integration contracts
The shapes the UI consumes, typed and quantified.
I do not specify a backend. I do specify the exact contracts every data-driven affordance reads, so a prototype builder never has to guess a formula, a threshold, a field, or a degraded path. Each contract below is build-ready: typed inputs, named units, explicit null-handling, and a stated default for every open item it closes.
H1 · Reorder recommendation
One formula. One unit. One degraded path.
The recommended reorder quantity on S102 and S004 is a single typed formula, in units, rounded to the brand pack or case size:
recommended_qty = round_up_to_pack( max(0, ceil(velocity_30d × lead_time_days) &minus; on_hand) )
velocity_30d = POS sell-through units in the trailing 30 days, as units/day (a float).
lead_time_days = brand-supplied integer; falls back to 14 when the brand omits it.
on_hand = live POS units from the stock contract (H3); the masked value, never a guess.
round_up_to_pack rounds up to the brand pack or case size; result is an integer unit count.
No-POS degraded path : I suppress the qty number and rank lines by order-history recency and frequency only. Under 2 orders I show Reorder? in place of a number, never a fabricated quantity.
Dead-line cutoff : a line is dead when sell_through_units(90d) == 0 (POS) or no reorder in 2 buying cycles (no POS). Dead lines de-emphasize and offer Remove, per F6 .
The "why" string is a typed template with explicit null-handling, not free text: &lcub;soldQty&rcub; sold in &lcub;windowDays&rcub;d · last ordered &lcub;monthsAgo&rcub;mo ago · &lcub;seasonFlag&rcub; . The sold clause drops when POS is absent; the last-ordered clause drops when the buyer has never ordered the line; seasonFlag is one of in season / off season / null, and null drops its clause and separator.
H2 · Photo-match confidence
One score scale. Three branchable states. (Resolves OI-09.)
Every candidate on S104 carries a score on a 0.00&ndash;1.00 scale. The three states the spec card lists become explicit branches off two thresholds and one cap:
Show a candidate when score &ge; 0.40 . Below that it is never rendered.
Best guess chip renders when the top score is < 0.65 ; the list still shows, flagged tentative.
No-match route : when no candidate clears 0.40 or count == 0 , I drop the list and route to the AI ingest shortcuts.
Cap at 5 candidates, ranked by descending score; ties break on brand-name sort.
H3 · POS stock & staleness
Per location, not per shop. (Resolves OI-02.)
The Stock Check Row on S004 and S102 consumes one typed object, per Shopify, Lightspeed, or Square location : &lcub; on_hand:int, location_id, location_name, synced_at, source_vendor &rcub; .
Granularity is per location. A multi-store buyer sees a location picker on the row; H1 reads the selected location's on_hand .
Refresh is webhook-driven where supported, else poll. Same shape either way.
Other (open API) vendors must supply this exact shape to render a live row; anything short renders as Last counted.
Strictly additive : a disconnected, stale, or absent POS never blocks ordering.
Staleness keys on age = now &minus; synced_at : LIVE when age < 15 min ; STALE ("as of {relative}") when 15 min &le; age < 24 h ; LAST COUNTED (manual-count path) when age &ge; 24 h or no successful sync.
H4 · Sync & conflict
Cart lines pick explicitly. Everything else last-write-wins.
CART fields : line qty, line variant, line add/remove, line note. Conflicts route to S807 .
NON-CART fields : draft title, ship-to selection, memo text. Last-write-wins, silently.
qty conflict = explicit pick. A scalar never silently keeps both; the buyer chooses local or server.
line-add = union. edit-vs-delete = conflict, routed to S807 , never auto-resolved.
"Keep both" means duplicate the line, both retained; offered only where duplication is coherent, never on a scalar qty.
Submit-pending conflict : if a cart-line conflict exists at Submit, I block and present S807 before S204 .
H5 · Backend contracts the UI assumes. Consumed, not implemented. Each row states the shape the design reads and, where it closes an open item, the chosen default.
Telemetry payload
One event shape. Every affordance in §07-G emits &lcub; event:string, source:enum, entity_id:string &rcub; , where source is one of barcode / photo / manual for scans and cart / order / brand for lifecycle. A value is never carried in the payload.
